SAN JIANG REVOLT: DELAYED IGNITION MODEL
(Canon direction: not immediate; begins as erosion)

High-level shape (agreed)
- The revolt is not an immediate consequence of the arena event.
- It is delayed and accumulative.
- The ignition is systemic: patrol attrition, confidence loss, and failed conscription attempts.

Stages (implementation-usable)
1) Ambient destabilization
   - rumors spread inconsistently
   - official statements conflict but converge on “Court won” and “dissent = execution”
   - patrols become more frequent/aggressive, then uneven

2) Quiet disappearance of small patrols
   - not presented as “heroic resistance” yet
   - treated as unexplained absences
   - community learns it outnumbers the state and can outthink its routines

3) Late alarm at the High Courts
   - signals arrive too late for orderly counteraction
   - response patterns are slow, procedural, and mismatched to street-level dynamics

4) Forced conscription: the inflection point
   - conscription raids are violently denied
   - authority loses its illusion of inevitability
   - guards show poor training and fear once compliance breaks

5) “Too late” phase
   - control collapses locally before it collapses politically
   - information spreads via disguised channels (work talk, prayer-like cadence, gossip that sounds harmless)

Michio’s placement (agreed narrative approach)
- For long stretches, this is not “Michio’s story.”
- He returns at a chosen climax point (e.g., staged execution climax).
- The world is allowed to exist and move without the protagonist centered.
