AGENCY COLLAPSE AND THE GREAT ECLIPSE
(High-level canon framing)

Core statement
- The Great Eclipse is framed as an agency-collapse event: it emerges when enough people stop meaningfully choosing.
- The Eclipse is not “a button press” or a purely cosmic spectacle; it is a systemic consequence of collective abdication of intent.

Why this framing exists in-universe
- The Technogarch-era failure is not “machines rebelled,” but “machines succeeded too well”:
  - friction removed
  - uncertainty removed
  - consequence-lag removed
  - judgment replaced by procedure
- As decision-making is externalized, lived intent density drops. The world keeps functioning, but authorship disappears.
- The Eclipse becomes “unbinding” (time losing stable anchoring) rather than “destruction.”

Narrative implications
- Repetition: The Eclipse can recur because the condition is societal/systemic, not a one-time villain act.
- Variability: Each Eclipse can look different because its surface expression depends on local structures and what forms agency-collapse takes.
- Causality tone: No single actor “causes” it, yet many contribute.

Real-world resonance (non-allegorical)
- Past: collapses via bureaucratic inertia, enforced “order” replacing moral responsibility.
- Present: “it’s policy” replacing ethics; metrics replacing judgment; algorithmic delegation normalizing non-authorship.
- Future: predictive governance and optimization cultures pushing toward risk-elimination mistaken for wisdom.
