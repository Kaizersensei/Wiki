# Prologue — Hirogawa

Michio sat on the edge of the waterfall cliff, legs dangling above the mist.

The sun had not fully risen yet. Its light was dim, but warm, cutting softly through the cold blue haze rising from the water below. Far beneath him, Hirogawa rested quietly—fragile from this height. Small. Close enough to protect. Far enough to watch over.

*I climbed here to watch the sunrise,* he thought.  
*I’ve been doing this since I was a child.*

The memory came easily.

---

The inner gardens of Ishigane. A koi pond by the gates. Five-year-old Michio stood beside his mother, Hitomi, her hand resting on his head—steady, familiar.

*She took me here all the time,* he remembered.  
*Afterwards, we’d visit the Daimyo and report anything unusual.*

Hitomi stood tall in his memory, binoculars raised, posture alert. Even then, she carried herself like someone trained to notice what others missed.

*She used to be a spy when the Seigetsu war was still burning,* Michio thought.  
*Ishigane knew those eyes were sharp enough to keep old war ghosts from wandering too close to the village.*

---

The image shifted.

Ishigane—still young then—kneeling as a samurai beneath the Emperor of Seigetsu. Honors bestowed. Steel and ceremony. The scene dissolved into fields, rooftops, rivers. Hirogawa, taking shape.

*He founded this village thirty-five years ago,* Michio reflected.  
*For ending conflicts no one wanted to remember, he was given this land—to build something neutral. Something meant to hold the Crimson tensions at bay.*

---

Another memory surfaced, lighter this time.

His parents—young, careless, human.

Hitomi and Hanzo spying on each other. Watching rooftops. Pretending not to notice. Laughing when caught. It ended with a proud Hanzo beside a pregnant Hitomi, his hand resting awkwardly on her belly.

*Mom was a spy. Dad was an investigator,* Michio thought.  
*Somehow they ended up snooping on each other… and liking what they found. I guess they wanted out of war. For me.*

---

The Daimyo’s mansion.

“Nothing unusual, Ishigane-dono,” Hitomi said, professional as ever.

“Thank you for your service,” Ishigane replied. “Proceed with your daily routine.”

Michio stepped forward before either could stop him.

“Gran’pa Ishi—can Kazuya come play outside?”

Hitomi froze.

“M-Michio—! I—I’m so sorry, Ishigane-dono!”

For a heartbeat, Ishigane only stared.

Then he laughed.

It was loud. Genuine. Brief.

*This was one of the few times I ever saw him laugh,* Michio remembered.  
*In that moment, you could see the joy still buried beneath years of war.*

Ishigane straightened.

“No,” he said calmly. “He skipped his chores yesterday. Triple duty today.”

Michio sighed. “Fine… I’ll see what the Sage is up to…”

He turned and ran, disappointment written in every step.

Behind him, Hitomi smiled awkwardly. “Always defiant… like his father.”

Ishigane said nothing.

Michio heard it anyway.

---

He walked upward, away from the mansion, toward the cliff plateau where the great tree stood.

Roots twisted through stone and earth, climbing skyward. The Infinitree rose higher than the mountain itself—a silent guardian the village seemed to have grown around rather than beneath.

With every jump, every climb, memory followed him.

Five-year-old Michio sat on Hanzo’s shoulders. Lanterns swayed. Music drifted. A festival alive with color and noise. Michio pointed excitedly at something unseen.

Clouds formed familiar shapes. Faces appeared in water reflections. Old laughter hid in fields of flowers and patches of greenery.

*Now that I think about it,* Michio thought, *there weren’t many children like us in the village.*

Kazuya. Himself.

*We were built differently. Warrior children.*

The thought unsettled him.

*Children going to war. Carrying the flags of their parents. Killing strangers without knowing their names or their struggles…*

He exhaled.

*I don’t understand war.*

A pause.

*And thankfully, I’ve never fought one.*  
*Not with other people.*  
*Only with my memories.*

---

Michio reached the foot of the Infinitree.

From below, its height was overwhelming. Roots like walls. Bark like cliffs. A small wooden door rested at the base of the trunk. Michio knocked.

“Yes?” a voice came from behind the door. “Is something wrong?”

Michio hesitated. “…Kinda. I mean…”

The door opened just enough for the Sage to step out. He closed it immediately behind him—careful, deliberate—leaving the quiet sense that something remained hidden within the tree.

“I need to gather herbs,” the Sage said. “Walk with me. Maybe you can tell me what’s troubling you.”

---

They walked through the wilderness.

Later, they sat beneath a tree’s shade, tea steaming gently between them. Their conversation was never heard—only gestures, pauses, shared silence. Whatever was said, it was left for the observer to piece together.

When they returned, the Sage unrolled an old scroll. Wooden forms. Jointed limbs. The mannequin design of Kodomaru.

“I suppose,” the Sage murmured, “we all feel lonely… and displaced… sometimes.”

---

That evening, Michio returned home.

Something wasn’t right.

The lights were on—and yet, no one was home.

“Mom?”  
His voice echoed back at him.  
“Mom?!”

He searched every room. Nothing.

*Somehow,* he thought, *an empty house felt worse than finding thieves.*  
*It was eerie. Unfamiliar. Scary.*

---

He ran to Ishigane.

A trader had come through the village that day.

“They carried rumors,” Ishigane said, stern and wounded. “Said Hanzo was seen jumping from a cliff near the Temple of Stillness. When pressed, they admitted the story’s been spreading for weeks.”

A pause.

“Your mother believed none of it,” Ishigane continued. “She wanted to see the body. She went alone… and did not return. Our search party found nothing. No sign of struggle. No answers.”

His voice lowered.

“I don’t know what to tell you, young man—except this: stay strong. The village is with you.”

---

*And that’s how I became an orphan… thirteen years ago...*

...Through the stubbornness of my parents...
