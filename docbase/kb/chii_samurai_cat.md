# Chii — Samurai Cat

**Species:** Feline (Eddaran animal-kin)

**Affiliation:** Eddara (Outer Territories)

**Status:** Wanderer / Retainer-for-hire

---

## Overview

Chii is a solitary samurai cat whose presence blends disciplined martial tradition with quiet eccentricity. Small of stature but alert in posture, he carries himself with the calm readiness of a seasoned blade. His black fur and piercing eyes give him a watchful, almost ceremonial appearance, softened only by his silence and deliberate movements.

One ear bears several metal piercings and rings — a personal marker rather than ornamentation, signifying oaths taken and paths survived. He wears a woven straw kasa adapted for his kind, pierced to allow his ears to pass through, marking him unmistakably as a traveler rather than a court retainer.

---

## Cultural Placement

By appearance and bearing alone, Chii is most readily associated with **Eddara**, among the sentient animal-kin who have adopted martial codes without abandoning their ancestral forms. His equipment and posture suggest exposure to Eastern sword traditions, likely learned through exile, pilgrimage, or service beyond Eddara’s borders.

Unlike many Eddaran guardians, Chii operates independently, implying either self-imposed exile or a role unsanctioned by the Elder Council. His presence near trade routes or border regions would not be unusual.

---

## Visual Identifiers

- Full black fur with subtle brown undertones in direct light
- Bright, focused eyes; expression permanently alert
- One ear pierced with multiple rings
- Straw kasa hat with ear openings
- Light samurai garb adapted for digitigrade stance
- No visible clan insignia

---

## Narrative Function

Chii is suited for:

- Optional ally or duelist encounter
- Wandering NPC offering trials, rumors, or rare techniques
- Silent observer during key Eddaran or Eastern political events

He does not explain himself unless pressed — and even then, only partially.

---

## Thematic Notes

Chii embodies restraint, chosen solitude, and the idea that honor can exist without banners or allegiance. His design reinforces Densetsu’s recurring theme that identity is forged through action, not origin.
