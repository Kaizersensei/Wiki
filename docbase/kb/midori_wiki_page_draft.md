# Midori — *The Blooming Shade*

> **Status:** Canon page draft (expanded).\
> **Scope:** Character sheet + first-arc relevance (Hirogawa).\
> **Notes:** This page avoids hard exposition; it favors implication and observable detail.

---

## Overview

Midori is one of **Okashira’s Eight Currents** within the **Ninth Wave**, known as the **Green Current** and titled **The Blooming Shade**. She is a **Wood-element general** (an elemental fusion of **Earth + Water → Wood**) whose warfare is defined less by brute force and more by **misdirection, psychological pressure, and battlefield control**.

Her first major appearance is tied to **Hirogawa’s Bamboo Thicket**, where enslaved workers are forced to mass-produce **Hagane Bamboo**—metal-infused cane used for fortification and armament.

---

## Role in the Story

### First Arc Function (Hirogawa)

Midori is designed to set the tone for antagonists early:

- The Ninth Wave are not faceless monsters; they are **sentient**, **human**, and internally motivated.
- Their strength is paired with **vulnerability**: conviction, doubt, memory, pride, fatigue, fear—things that don’t show on an insignia.
- Midori’s presence introduces a core story rule: **truth can be present and still be obscured**.

### Narrative Signals

Midori’s actions in Hirogawa establish:

- **Occupation through “order”**: labor turned into ritual, efficiency masquerading as necessity.
- **Control without spectacle**: she doesn’t need to shout; the space itself becomes her weapon.
- **Youth as a weapon and a wound**: she acts with a confidence that implies either extraordinary competence—or a generation taught that survival requires overconfidence.

## Appearance

Midori’s silhouette reads as **light**, **mobile**, and deliberately **unarmored**—a visual contradiction for a general.

**Key visual markers (canonical look):**

- Hair: **short dark-blue**, swept and tidy; a youthful cut that still feels tactical.
- Eyes: **green**, bright and steady.
- Cape: **fox fur mantle** over a long cape; inner lining is **emerald green fabric** (silk-like).
- Outfit: **minimal, practical, dancer-like mobility**—designed for rapid pivots and vertical movement.
- Palette: greens and browns; “forest dweller,” rather than military.

Her presence often feels like **a clean shape moving through messy terrain**—as if she doesn’t belong to the mud and wetlands, yet controls them.

---

## Personality

Midori reads as:

- **Composed** under pressure, amused rather than threatened.
- **Curious** in a predatory way—interested in people who don’t behave as expected.
- **Strategic**: she prefers outcomes that leave her options open.

She is not written as “evil for evil’s sake.” Instead, her cruelty (when present) is framed as **procedure**—something she learned to do, not something she celebrates.

---

## Abilities

Midori is an **Illusionist** whose Wood-affinity manifests as *living misdirection*—not just visuals, but space, footing, and attention.

### Combat Identity

- **Creates false certainty** (you commit to the wrong target, the wrong angle, the wrong ground).
- **Punishes aggression** and rewards patience.
- **Uses the environment** (bamboo pillars/logs/cover) as part of her “spellcasting.”

### Signature Techniques (canonical mechanics naming)

- **Log Substitution** — short teleport anchored to bamboo/log points; may leave a fragile clone behind.
- **Ambush Teleport** — reappears behind the player or near high-value interactables.
- **Star Shard Barrage** — aerial fan/cone of sharpened wood projectiles.
- **Love Spell (AOE)** — brief paralyze/stun field used to create openings.
- **Wood Golem (Ultimate)** — summons or becomes a massive wood threat that changes arena priorities.

## Chapter 1 — Bamboo Thicket Incident

### Situation

Bamboo workers go missing. The thicket’s outer wetlands appear calm—until Michio discovers:

- workers **chained**, exhausted,
- Hagane Bamboo being produced in an **unnatural volume**,
- green-clad Ninth Wave squads supervising with casual discipline,
- structures deeper in the thicket implying a **fortification project**.

### The Hostage Pattern

Midori keeps one worker separate—her **private slave/hostage**—introducing a constraint:

- A fight becomes a moral problem *before* it becomes a skill check.
- Even victory can be framed as failure if it costs the bystander.

### “Perfect Run” Intent

In the canonical baseline, Michio’s confrontation with Midori establishes:

- he can read the battlefield without taking the bait,
- he acts decisively **without losing compassion**,
- he survives her misdirection *and* denies her the clean narrative of “you are no different.”

## Affiliation

- **Faction:** Ninth Wave
- **Rank:** One of the Eight Currents
- **Division:** Green Current (Wood)

Midori commands squads that appear lightly equipped, fast, and confident—forces trained to win by **controlling perception** as much as territory.

## Themes and Subtext

Midori is a vehicle for:

- **Generational fracture** — the young being shaped into weapons by “older inevitabilities.”
- **Humanization of antagonists** — enemies with interiority, not just function.
- **War as procedure** — cruelty performed as routine, not rage.

## Open Questions / Hooks (intentional gaps)

These are deliberately left unresolved for later reveals:

- **Recruitment:** conscription, debt, oath, seduction, ideology—what made her *choose* the Ninth Wave?
- **Origin:** where she came from; what she lost before she gained power.
- **Age:** visually young; whether that is literal, cultural, or temporal.
- **Private hostage:** why she keeps one worker close (leverage? mirror? punishment? necessity?).
