# Crimson Dynasty

## Overview
The **Crimson Dynasty** is a centralized authoritarian state defined by ritualized order, managed scarcity, and controlled visibility. Its authority does not rely on constant violence, but on predictable enforcement, bureaucratic fear, and the careful shaping of public narrative.

The Dynasty presents itself as ancient, stable, and inevitable. Internally, it governs through layered courts, selective spectacle, and aggressive resource control—most critically, water.

---

## Geography & Regions

### San Jiang (Capital)
A cliff-carved administrative city built around a constrained river system. The city is vertically layered, with strict separation between governance, labor, and spectacle.

* Key features:
  * Tiered districts
  * Engineered river bottlenecks
  * Arena-centered judicial culture
  * Controlled illumination via Ki-infused infrastructure

### Riverside Low Districts
Dense, overpopulated slums along the river’s lower flow.

* High anonymity
* Heavy patrol fluctuation
* Ideal for disappearing, not organizing

### Agrarian Outlands
Downstream rice-growing regions affected by increasing salinity.

* Quietly over-taxed
* Increasing water dependence
* Framed as “self-sufficient” while steadily collapsing

### Resource Corridors
Bridges, wells, and river checkpoints treated as strategic assets.

* Guarded more reliably than people
* Movement shaped through infrastructure, not walls

---

## Points of Interest

* **The Arena of San Jiang**  
  Judicial spectacle space where authority is legitimized through ritual combat.

* **Central Courts Platform**  
  Open-air ruling complex with masked judges, designed for visibility and ceremony rather than deliberation.

* **River Constriction Gates**  
  Engineered choke points regulating flow, access, and transport.

* **Monitored Wells**  
  Inspected before civilians during patrol cycles; water levels logged obsessively.

---

## Systemic Notes

* Water access precedes all governance decisions.
* Fire is used for displacement, not annihilation.
* Evacuation and seizure are preferred to mass killing.
* Control operates best when it appears administrative rather than violent.
