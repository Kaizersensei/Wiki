## Okashira and the Kiputer — Inevitability of Control

Yes—**naturally, and inevitably.** Okashira seeking access to the **Kiputer** is not incidental; it cements his role in the cosmology of *Densetsu*.

---

### Ideological Alignment (but Incomplete Understanding)

Okashira believes in **control through certainty**.

- He rejects chaos, ambiguity, and lived contradiction.
- He views suffering as a mismanaged system rather than a human condition.
- Like the Technogarchs, he believes the world fails because it is *allowed to choose*.

To him, the Kiputer represents the ultimate instrument to:
- eliminate uncertainty,
- pre-justify domination,
- replace moral choice with calculated necessity.

What Okashira misunderstands is fatal: he sees the Kiputer’s collapse as a failure of execution, not of premise.

---

### The Kiputer as a “Silent God”

Unlike gods, spirits, or the Chronobonsai, the Kiputer:

- does not judge,
- does not remember,
- does not care.

To Okashira, this absence of judgment is purity. Where gods failed to save Eddara and balance failed to protect the weak, the Kiputer promises a future where **mercy is irrelevant**.

> “If suffering is inevitable, then let it at least be efficient.”

---

### Contrast With the Bonsai Sage

This establishes a philosophical triangle:

- **Bonsai Sage** — observes, predicts, intervenes minimally.
- **Chronobonsai / Infinitree** — stabilize time through accumulated lived memory.
- **Kiputer** — deletes memory in favor of outcome certainty.
- **Okashira** — seeks results without accountability.

The Sage predicts futures.
Okashira seeks to overwrite them.

---

### Why Okashira Can Never Truly Use the Kiputer

The Kiputer cannot process heroes.

- It cannot simulate defiance born of compassion.
- It cannot resolve futures that hinge on choosing loss over optimization.

Even with partial access, Okashira would receive:
- contradictory outputs,
- unstable projections,
- futures that collapse the moment he acts on them.

To Okashira, this appears as the world resisting correction. To the universe, it is the Kiputer rejecting him—just as it rejected its creators.

---

### Narrative Payoff

Okashira becomes not a caricatured tyrant, but the **last inheritor of Technogarch hubris**.

He is not trying to break time.
He is trying to *finish what the Technogarchs started*—and proves, by doing so, why it must never be finished.

---

### In-Universe Summary

> The Technogarchs tried to remove choice from the future.
> Okashira tries to remove responsibility from the present.
> Both mistake certainty for salvation.
